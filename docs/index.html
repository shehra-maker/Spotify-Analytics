02

Energy vs. Popularity

Does higher energy actually predict higher popularity? A common assumption, tested directly.

Finding — The trend line slopes downward. Splitting tracks into energy quartiles: the lowest-energy quartile averages 42.1 popularity; the highest-energy quartile averages 34.9 — a 7-point gap in the opposite direction from what intuition suggests. Correlation: -0.105.

What Actually Correlates with Popularity

Every audio feature, ranked. Cross-validated identically in SQL and Excel (CORREL formulas).

Finding — Duration is the strongest single predictor (-0.139, shorter = more popular) — stronger than energy. No feature exceeds |0.14|, meaning no single acoustic property is a strong predictor on its own; genre/subgenre context carries more weight than any individual feature.
04

Songs Are Getting Shorter

A widely discussed industry trend, independently verified in this data.

Finding — Average duration dropped from 4.49 min (1980s) to 3.24 min (2020s) — over a full minute shorter. Generally attributed to streaming economics (shorter songs mean more completions per listening hour) and playlist/algorithm optimization. The 2020s sample is smaller (626 tracks) — directional, not definitive.
05

Recommendations

Full write-up: INSIGHTS.md

  1. Don't use "high energy" as a proxy for "engaging" in a recommendation system — the relationship is weak and negative, not positive.
  2. Duration is the strongest lever available — shorter tracks trend more popular, consistent with the real industry-wide shortening trend.
  3. Treat individual audio features as weak signals — no feature exceeds a 0.14 correlation; genre/subgenre context matters more than any single acoustic property.
  4. Correlation isn't causation — EDM's low popularity and high energy could run through genre rather than energy directly; a within-genre check would be the rigorous next step.
